About the project

A resource built around understanding

My name is Houda, and I am a student in the Educational Support Program at NBCC. I created this website as an educational project to share clear, accessible information about dyslexia and mutism.

🌱 The purpose

Students learn and communicate in different ways. Understanding their experiences can help adults respond with patience, respect, and appropriate support.

🤝 Who can use it?

Parents, teachers, educational assistants, support staff, and anyone who wants to learn more about supporting students.

My goal: to focus on the student as a whole person—not simply on a diagnosis or label—and to encourage individualized, inclusive support.
Topic 01

📖 Dyslexia

Understanding reading and language-based learning differences.

What is dyslexia?

Dyslexia is a specific learning disorder that primarily affects reading and spelling skills. It is not a reflection of a person's intelligence. Students with dyslexia may need explicit, systematic and appropriately paced instruction.

What might we notice?

  • Difficulty with accurate or fluent word reading
  • Difficulty connecting letters and sounds
  • Spelling challenges
  • Difficulty reading unfamiliar words
  • Fatigue or frustration during reading tasks

🏫 Helpful classroom supports

  • Give clear, step-by-step instructions.
  • Use explicit instruction in sound-letter relationships.
  • Allow appropriate extra time when needed.
  • Provide accessible text or text-to-speech when appropriate.
  • Check understanding without putting the student on the spot.

💛 Support the learner

Recognize effort and progress. Avoid assuming that reading difficulty means low ability. Provide opportunities for students to demonstrate knowledge in more than one way.

Important: Classroom observations do not diagnose dyslexia. Identification and diagnosis should involve qualified professionals and appropriate assessment.
Topic 02

💬 Mutism

Understanding when speaking or verbal communication is difficult or absent.

What does mutism mean?

Mutism can describe a situation in which a person does not speak. Selective mutism is a specific condition in which a child consistently cannot speak in particular social situations despite speaking in other situations. It is important not to assume that silence is simply a choice or defiance.

What might we notice?

  • Speaking comfortably in some settings but not others
  • Using gestures, writing, pointing, or facial expressions instead of speech
  • Difficulty answering questions aloud
  • Visible anxiety around speaking situations
  • Communication that changes depending on the environment

🏫 Helpful classroom supports

  • Do not force the student to speak.
  • Accept non-verbal communication.
  • Reduce unnecessary pressure and public attention.
  • Build predictable, trusting routines.
  • Offer gradual opportunities for communication with appropriate professional guidance.

💛 Build safety and trust

Give the student time. Treat communication attempts as meaningful, whether they are spoken or non-verbal. Coordinate support with families and qualified professionals when needed.

Important: A quiet student is not automatically experiencing selective mutism. Assessment requires consideration of the student's communication across settings and input from appropriate professionals.
For families

👨‍👩‍👧 For Parents

Small changes in understanding can make a big difference.

Listen

Ask your child what helps them learn or communicate comfortably. Give them time to respond.

Collaborate

Share observations with teachers and support staff. Consistent communication can help adults work toward common goals.

Celebrate progress

Focus on strengths, effort, growth, and participation—not only on difficulties.

For schools

👩‍🏫 For Teachers & Educational Support Staff

Practical principles for an inclusive environment.

Observe

Look for patterns across activities, people, and environments rather than making assumptions from one moment.

Adapt

Use appropriate accommodations and multiple ways for students to access information and demonstrate learning.

Collaborate

Work with families, school teams, and qualified professionals when a student needs additional support.

Inclusive mindset: Ask “What support would help this student participate?” rather than “Why can't this student do it the usual way?”
