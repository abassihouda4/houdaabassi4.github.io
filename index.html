About the project

A resource built around understanding

My name is Houda, and I am a student in the Educational Support Program at NBCC. I created this website to share clear, accessible information that can help families, teachers, and educational support staff understand communication differences and support students in the classroom.

🌱 Purpose

Students learn and communicate in different ways. Understanding communication needs can help adults respond with patience, respect, and appropriate support.

🤝 Who is this for?

Parents, teachers, educational assistants, educational support staff, and anyone who wants to learn more about supporting students.

Inclusive approach: A communication difficulty does not define a student's intelligence, personality, or potential. Supports should be based on the individual student's needs, strengths, and goals.
Topic 01 • Language

💬 Developmental Language Disorder (DLD)

Understanding & Using Language

What is DLD?

Developmental Language Disorder is a condition involving persistent difficulties with language that can affect everyday communication and/or learning. Difficulties can involve understanding language, expressing ideas, vocabulary, sentence structure, or using language in different situations.

DLD is identified through professional assessment. Classroom observations alone do not diagnose DLD.

🇨🇦 Canadian context

7.1%

A Canadian estimate frequently cited in professional and university resources is approximately 7.1% of children having Developmental Language Disorder.

Prevalence estimates can vary depending on the population, age group, diagnostic criteria, and methodology used.

🔍 Possible characteristics

  • Difficulty finding the right words
  • Difficulty understanding complex language or instructions
  • Limited or slower vocabulary development
  • Difficulty forming or understanding sentences
  • Difficulty explaining or organizing ideas
  • Difficulty remembering or processing verbal information

🚧 Possible classroom barriers

  • Long or multi-step instructions
  • Fast-paced classroom discussions
  • New or unfamiliar vocabulary
  • Explaining answers verbally
  • Group work that depends heavily on language
  • Understanding figurative or complex language

💪 Possible strengths

Students with DLD have individual strengths that may appear in areas such as visual learning, creativity, practical activities, problem-solving, interests, or non-language-based tasks.

Remember: strengths vary from student to student.

👩‍🏫 Classroom support

  • Use short, clear instructions.
  • Break instructions into smaller steps.
  • Use pictures, gestures, diagrams, and written keywords.
  • Pre-teach important vocabulary.
  • Allow extra processing time.
  • Check understanding in a supportive way.

Topic 02 • Speech

🗣️ Speech Sound Disorder (SSD) – Lisping

Speech Sounds & Pronunciation

What is a Speech Sound Disorder?

A Speech Sound Disorder affects a child's ability to produce speech sounds accurately or clearly. Speech sound difficulties can involve sound substitutions, omissions, additions, or distortions and may affect how easily a child is understood.

👄 What is lisping?

Lisping is a speech sound difficulty in which some sounds may be produced differently from the expected speech pattern. The /s/ and /z/ sounds are commonly discussed when describing lisping.

Lisping is best presented as an example of a speech sound difficulty rather than as a separate broad diagnostic category.

🇨🇦 Canadian research

Recent Canadian research has examined speech sound disorders in children, including how SSD can be assessed in multilingual children and how speech production involves coordinated movements of the jaw, lips, and tongue.

Important: A recent national Canadian prevalence estimate specifically for lisping was not identified in the sources reviewed.

🔍 Possible characteristics

  • Difficulty producing certain speech sounds
  • Sound substitutions or distortions
  • Speech that may be harder for unfamiliar listeners to understand
  • Difficulty with specific sounds such as /s/ or /z/ in some cases
  • Possible frustration during communication

🚧 Possible classroom barriers

  • Reading aloud
  • Oral presentations
  • Speaking in large groups
  • Being misunderstood by peers or adults
  • Feeling pressure to repeat speech

💪 Possible strengths

A speech sound difficulty does not determine a student's intelligence or academic potential. Students may have strong abilities in written communication, creativity, visual learning, problem-solving, or other areas.

Strengths vary from student to student.

👩‍🏫 Classroom strategies

👂 Listen Focus on the student's message.
⏳ Wait Give enough time to finish speaking.
❤️ Encourage Create a safe communication environment.
📝 Offer options Use written or alternative ways to participate when appropriate.
🙅 Don't pressure Avoid unnecessary repeated requests to speak again.

For educators & educational support staff

👩‍🏫 Inclusive Communication in the Classroom

Small changes can make communication more accessible without lowering expectations.

1. Make language visible

Use visuals, written keywords, examples, gestures, and clear routines to support spoken information.

2. Give processing time

Pause after asking a question and allow the student enough time to understand, plan, and respond.

3. Offer ways to participate

When appropriate, allow written responses, visual supports, partner work, or smaller-group participation.

4. Protect dignity

Do not draw unnecessary attention to speech or language differences. Avoid teasing, imitation, or public correction.

5. Focus on communication

Listen to what the student is trying to communicate rather than focusing only on errors.

6. Work as a team

When concerns persist, communicate with families and the appropriate school or speech-language professionals.

People-first approach: A student is a whole person first. Use respectful language and individualized supports rather than defining a student only by a diagnosis or communication difficulty.
For families

👨‍👩‍👧 Supporting Communication at Home

Families can support communication through everyday interactions, encouragement, and collaboration with professionals.

💬 For children with DLD

  • Talk about everyday activities.
  • Read and discuss books together.
  • Introduce new words in meaningful contexts.
  • Give your child time to answer.
  • Break complicated instructions into smaller steps.
  • Share concerns with the school and speech-language professional when appropriate.

🗣️ For speech sound difficulties

  • Listen patiently and avoid interrupting.
  • Show interest in what your child is saying.
  • Avoid teasing or making fun of speech errors.
  • Encourage communication rather than focusing only on pronunciation.
  • Ask a speech-language pathologist for guidance when concerns persist.
Remember: This website provides general educational information. It is not a diagnostic tool and does not replace assessment or advice from a qualified speech-language pathologist or other appropriate professional.
